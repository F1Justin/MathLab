# Probability-space WT laws with square-integrable windows and tables

Let \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\) be any probability space. Assume real windows and tables are jointly measurable, with finite mean squared \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) norms. Impose the original common supports, unit mass of almost every window, unit plateau of almost every table, nonnegative mean autocorrelation, and mean covering at least one on the original interval. Then the [simultaneous support-radius and actual-cost theorem](simultaneous_radius.md) holds with exactly the same conclusions, including \(o_A(R^{-2})\) autocorrelation error. This allows continuous joint laws and individual functions in \(L^2\) on their fixed supports.

Here is the direct Hilbert-space argument. Let \(\mathcal H=L^2(\Omega;\mathbb C)\), let \(w(x),t(x)\) denote the random functions as vectors in \(\mathcal H\) almost everywhere, and use the **bilinear** contraction \([f,h]=\mathbb E(fh)\) for the Fourier-side cover. The Hilbert norm, which involves complex conjugation, is used for the autocorrelation. The spatial vector functions can be taken strongly measurable: square-integrable product-measurable functions are limits in \(L^2(\Omega\times\mathbb R)\) of finite linear combinations of rectangle indicators on bounded spatial intervals. This also restricts their ranges to a separable Hilbert subspace. Reflection may be incorporated by taking the product probability space with an independent fair sign that simultaneously reflects both spatial functions.

The centred frequency vector \(P(z)=\int e^{-izx}w(x)\,dx\) is an entire \(\mathcal H\)-valued function by bounded common support and Cauchy–Schwarz. It satisfies \(P(0)=\mathbf1\), \(\|P(t)\|^2=\widehat A(t)\) on the real axis, and the same Plancherel derivative estimates as in the [cubic Fourier bound](universal_radius_rate.md). All estimates involving the norm, its regularizations, and the signed scalar square root in that proof apply in a Hilbert space. In particular the chosen \(V_R\) and its primitive estimate depend only on \(A\) and the estimates there.

For each fixed spectral cutoff in the [table construction](simultaneous_radius.md), replace the atomwise kernels by the Hilbert-valued kernel \(\widehat K(t)=\chi_N(t)P(t)/g(t)\). At a real zero \(t_0\) of \(g\) of order \(k\), \(\|P(t)\|=O(|t-t_0|^k)\). The norm-convergent Taylor expansion gives \(P^{(j)}(t_0)=0\) for \(j<k\), so the quotient extends analytically across the zero. The smooth compactly supported frequency kernel therefore has Schwartz spatial decay in Hilbert norm. Put
\[
 U_L(x)=\int [K(y),t_L(x-y)]\,dy .
\]
Joint reflection gives a scalar real even table. The Fourier identities and both projections in the finite proof use only \(|[a,b]|\le\|a\|\|b\|\) and Plancherel. In the plateau estimate \(m(t)\) becomes \([\mathbf1,P(t)]/g(t)\), with \(|m|\le1\) and \(m(0)=1\). The shoulder difference \(t_L-\mathbf1 I_L\) has bounded spatial \(L^1(\mathcal H)\) and \(L^2(\mathcal H)\) norms uniformly in \(L\), by the bounded total length of the two translated shoulders and finite mean energy. Its convolution with \(K\) thus has the required tail and derivative estimates. The reflected mean covering has fixed continuous shoulder profiles: continuity follows from translation continuity and Cauchy–Schwarz in \(L^2(\mathbb R;\mathcal H)\), and the plateau and support give the constant exterior values. From this point, the window, table, deficit envelopes, and energy corrections are scalar and the proof applies verbatim.

The original Sidon application uses finite joint laws of bounded functions. The probability-space version above is a mathematical extension of the witness class. [Carter–O'Bryant–Tao, Definition 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36207v1#S2.SS2)
