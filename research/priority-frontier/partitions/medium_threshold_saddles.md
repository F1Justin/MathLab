# Angular minima at intermediate block thresholds

Write \(B(z)=\sum_{k\ge j}z^k/k!\), \(H=zB'\), \(K=zH'\). The [verifier](check_medium_threshold_saddles.py) treats the intermediate integers \(9\le j\le127\) for the [whole-row argument](fixed_threshold_rows.md). At each radius the required upper minimizer of \(\Re B(re^{it})\) is unique, lies strictly between \(0\) and \(\pi/j\), and has \(H<0,\ \Re K<0,\ \Im B>0\). Here \(H<0\) includes reality. The two rays in radius admit analytic estimates. For the bounded gap the main test involves only the first four moments of a scalar conditional Poisson variable. A direct angle enclosure handles the remaining subintervals.

## Two intervals uniform in the threshold

For \(j\ge4\) and \(0<r\le(j+1)/8\), put \(e=r/(j+1)\) and write the normalized series as \(e^{ijt}G(e^{it})\), where \(G=\sum_{k\ge0}a_k z^k\), \(a_0=1,\ a_1=e,\ a_{k+1}/a_k\le e\). Its autocorrelations \(c_d=\sum_{k\ge0}a_ka_{k+d}\) satisfy \(c_d/c_1\le e^{d-1}\) for \(d\ge1\). Since \(|\sin(dt)|\le d\sin t\) on \(0<t<\pi\) and \(\sum_{d\ge2}d^2(1/8)^{d-1}<1\), the derivative of \(|G(e^{it})|^2=c_0+2\sum_{d\ge1}c_d\cos(dt)\) is strictly negative there.

The total argument \(jt+\arg G(e^{it})\) increases strictly, since \(|G-1|\le1/7\) and \(|dG/dt|\le e/(1-e)^2\). At \(t=\pi/j\), the first imaginary term \(e\sin t\) dominates the rest by \(\sum_{k\ge2} k e^{k-1}<1\). Thus the first crossing of the negative real ray occurs at \(0<t_c<\pi/j\). For \(t>t_c\) the strictly decreasing modulus forces \(\Re(e^{ijt}G(e^{it}))>\Re(e^{ijt_c}G(e^{it_c}))\); at \(t_c\) the real-part derivative is positive because the modulus derivative is negative. All minima on the upper semicircle therefore occur before \(t_c\), where the imaginary part of \(B\) is positive. Testing at \(\pi/j\) forces \(\cos(jt)\le-1+2/7<-0.7\) at every minimum. This condition cuts out a single interval before \(t_c\). On it the first term in both \(\Re H/j\) and \(\Re K/j^2\), after the same normalization, is \(\cos(jt)\), with error bounded by \(\sum_{k\ge1}(1+k/4)^2(1/8)^k<0.3\). Thus the real part is strictly convex there, its minimum is unique, and all claimed signs follow.

For \(r\ge\max(64,4j)\), the first \(j\) terms removed from the full exponential have absolute sum at most \(e^r e^{-0.4r}\). Indeed the Poisson lower-tail Chernoff bound at \(r/4\) gives exponent \(-r(3/4-\log(4)/4)<-0.4r\). Up to two logarithmic derivatives cost at most a factor \(r^2\). On the right semicircle put \(y=r\sin t,\ x_y=\sqrt{r^2-y^2}\) and test at \(y=\pi\), where \(x_\pi>r-1/8\). The left semicircle has real part at least \(-1-e^{0.6r}\). For \(y\ge2\pi\) on the right, the loss \(e^{x_\pi}-e^{x_{2\pi}}\ge e^{r-1/8}/(2r)>2e^{0.6r}\), since \(x_\pi-x_{2\pi}\ge3\pi^2/(2r)\). For \(y\le 2\pi\) outside \([3\pi/4,5\pi/4]\), \(\cos y\ge-1/\sqrt2\) also rules out a minimum. On the remaining interval \(0<t<4/r<\pi/j,\ x_y>r-1\), and \(\cos(y+t)\) and \(\cos(y+2t)\) are below \(-0.4\). The exponential terms \(ze^z,(z+z^2)e^z\) therefore give \(\Re H<-r e^{r-1}/3,\ \Re K<0\); the discarded derivative terms are bounded by \(r^2e^{0.6r}\). The minimum is unique there. At this stationary point \(H\) is real, and \(B=H/z-z^{j-1}/(j-1)!\) gives \(\Im B\ge e^{r-1}\sin(t)/3-e^{0.6r}>0\) since \(r\sin t\ge3\pi/4\).

## Excluding all other angles by four moments

Let \(X\) be Poisson with parameter \(r\) conditioned on \(X\ge j\), and put \(\phi(t)=\mathbb E e^{itX}=B(re^{it})/B(r)\). Denote the mean by \(\mu\), variance by \(v\), and third and fourth central moments by \(m_3,m_4\). For \(0\le t\le\pi\),
\[
 1-|\phi(t)|^2\ \ge\ \frac{v t^2}{1+K_0t^2},
 \qquad K_0=\frac{2[5(m_4/v+3v)+1]}{27}. \tag{1}
\]
To prove this, \(Y=X-X'\) for an independent copy \(X'\) is symmetric unimodal, because the original distribution is log-concave with interval support and convolution preserves this property. Its law is a mixture of discrete uniforms on \(\{-k,\ldots,k\}\). For a component write \(h=k(k+1)/3\) for its variance. Its fourth moment is \((9h^2-h)/5\), and
\[
 1-\mathbb E\cos(tY)\ \ge\ \frac{h t^2}{2(1+2h t^2/3)}
\]
on that component. Indeed for \(kt\ge2\pi\) the normalized Dirichlet kernel has modulus at most \(\pi/((2k+1)t)<1/4\). If \(\pi\le kt\le2\pi\), its numerator \(\sin((k+1/2)t)\) is either nonpositive or at most \(\sin(t/2)\), because the argument is in \([\pi,2\pi+t/2]\). Thus the kernel is at most \(1/(2k+1)\le1/5\) for \(k\ge2\); for \(k=1\) this range contains only \(t=\pi\), where the kernel is negative. Both ranges give a loss at least \(3/4\). For \(kt\le\pi\) and \(x=h t^2\le5\), the fourth-order cosine bound yields \((x/2)(1-3x/20)\ge x/[2(1+2x/3)]\). If \(kt\le\pi\) and \(x>5\), only \(k=1\) is possible, since \(k\ge2\) would give \(x\le\pi^2/2\). For \(k=1\) this range has \(t>\pi/2\), and the exact loss \(2(1-\cos t)/3\) exceeds \(2/3\), more than the required bound for \(x\le2\pi^2/3<12\). The atom \(k=0\) is immediate. Mixing and Cauchy–Schwarz give the bound with denominator \(1+2t^2\mathbb E h^2/(3\mathbb E h)\). Now \(\mathbb E h=2v\) and \(\mathbb E Y^4=2m_4+6v^2\); solving the component fourth-moment formula gives (1).

Choose \(0<\alpha<\pi<\beta\) and put \(a=\alpha/\mu,\ b=\beta/\mu\). The following strict scalar tests suffice.

1. Choose \(\gamma\ge\beta\) with \(\gamma^{-2}+K_0/\mu^2<\pi^{-2}\). Require \(\cos\alpha-va^2/2>-1+v(\pi/\mu)^2/2\). At \(t=\pi/\mu\) the trial real part is at most the right side of the second inequality. For \(0\le t\le a\) Taylor's inequality gives \(\Re\phi(t)\ge\cos\alpha-va^2/2\). On \(\beta\le c\le\gamma\) also require \(\cos c-v(c/\mu)^2/2>-1+v(\pi/\mu)^2/2\); the verifier checks this lower bound on intervals of length \(\pi/20\). For \(\gamma/\mu\le t\le\pi\), (1) and \(1-|\phi|\ge(1-|\phi|^2)/2\) force a strictly larger real part than the trial. Every minimizer lies in \((a,b)\).
2. For the size-biased laws with weights \(X\) and \(X^2\) let \(u_s,v_s\) be the mean and variance under weight \(X^s\). For \(s=1,2\) require \(b u_s<2\pi\) and \(\max(\cos(a u_s),\cos(b u_s))+b^2v_s/2<0\). The maximum of cosine on this interval occurs at an endpoint. The centered characteristic-function bound \(|\mathbb E_s e^{it(X-u_s)}-1|\le t^2v_s/2\) shows \(\Re H<0,\ \Re K<0\) throughout. Strict convexity gives a unique minimum and there \(H\) is real.
3. Require \(b^2(m_4/v+3v)<6\). Since \(y\sin(ty)\ge t y^2-t^3 y^4/6\) for real \(y\) and \(t\ge0\), the derivative of \(|\phi|^2\) is strictly negative on \((0,b]\). At the minimizer this derivative is \(2(H/B(r))\Im\phi\), hence \(\Im B>0\).
4. Either \(\mu/j>\beta/\pi\), or require \(\pi^2\mathbb E[X(X-j)^3]<6j^2\mathbb E[X(X-j)]\). In the first case \(b<\pi/j\). In the other case, whenever \(\pi/j\le b\), the last moment inequality gives \(\mathbb E[X\sin(\pi(X-j)/j)]>0\), so \((\Re\phi)'(\pi/j)>0\). Since \(a<\pi/j\) and the real part is strictly convex on the bracket, the minimum occurs before \(\pi/j\).

All expressions use moments only through order four. The [code](check_medium_threshold_saddles.py) checks these tests with outward rounding for an entire radius interval. For a fixed integer center \(c\) in that interval, \(\mathbb E[(X-c)_+^k]\) is nondecreasing and \(\mathbb E[(c-X)_+^k]\) nonincreasing as a function of \(r\), by monotone likelihood ratio. Their endpoint values therefore enclose every raw moment about \(c\) before conversion to centered moments. At a point radius evaluate \(Z=\sum_{i\ge0}j!r^i/(j+i)!\), so \(\Pr(X=j)=1/Z\). The series is stopped before \(X=512\); for \(r\le128\) the omitted sum is less than \(10^{-60}\) by the explicit factorial comparison in the code and successive ratios below \(1/2\).

The moments of the full law and of its restriction to \(X\ge c+1\) follow by the same exact summation identity. For a restriction to \(X\ge l\) write \(M_k=\mathbb E[(X-c)^k\,\boldsymbol1_{X\ge l}]\). Then
\[
 M_{k+1}=(r-c)M_k+r\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom ki M_i+l\Pr(X=l)(l-c)^k.
\]
This follows by shifting the series for \(X(X-c)^k\), including its lower boundary. The full norm and the finitely many probabilities through \(c+1\) initialize both recurrences. Subtraction gives the lower part. This evaluates the moments of the complete distribution even though a short series estimates its norm.

## The bounded radius verification

For \(9\le j\le127\) the script covers \([(j+1)/8,\min(128,\max(64,4j))]\). Beyond this interval either the large-radius estimate above or the [mean-128 criterion](poisson_explicit.md) applies, since \(\mu\ge r\). It first tries the four-moment test on a closed interval. Where a midpoint does not pass, the fallback brackets the first stationary angle by opposite strict signs of \(\Im H\), verifies \(\Re K<0\) on the bracket and compares \(\Re B\) on its complementary upper-semicircle arcs with an interior trial. Independent one-sided bisections then check \(\Re H<0,\ t<\pi/j,\ \Im B>0\) at the enclosed stationary point. All angle quantities and trial comparisons use Arb. The floating-point stationarity estimate only proposes a bracket. The method extends the [thresholds five through eight certificate](small_threshold_saddles.md).

For \(r\le j+1\) it factors out \(r^j/j!\) and uses Horner evaluation on the excess powers. The cutoff is increased until the bound \(2j!(j+M)^2(j+1)^M/(j+M)!<10^{-18}\) holds, with successive ratios below \(1/2\); this also bounds the compensated imaginary part \(B-H/j\) at the saddle. Larger radii use the exact finite Taylor complements of the exponential, multiplied by \(e^{-r}\). Every split preserves both endpoints. For the higher thresholds the scalar criterion covers the entire bounded interval without invoking any angular subdivisions.

The complete run covered all 119 thresholds with 10,636 moment-certified radius intervals and 581 angular fallback intervals. All of the latter belong to thresholds 9 through 20. The scalar proof covers the entire compact radius range for each threshold 21 through 127. Running the script with no arguments reproduces the complete calculation.
