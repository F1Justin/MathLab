# The global angular minimum at thresholds five through eight

For \(j=5,6,7,8\) the exponential tail \(B(z)=\sum_{k\ge j}z^k/k!\) satisfies (1) of the [Poisson saddle lemma](poisson_saddle.md) at every radius. Consequently the [whole-row theorem](fixed_threshold_rows.md) applies individually to each of these four thresholds. We prove the two unbounded ends analytically and use outward-rounded interval arithmetic on the compact interval between them. Write \(H=zB'\), \(K=zH'\), \(z=r e^{it}\).

For \(0<r\le1/16\), divide \(B\) by \(r^j/j!\) and set \(e=r/(j+1)\le1/96\), \(u=jt\). Its coefficients at powers \(j+k\) are \(1,e,\ldots\), bounded by \(e^k\). In the variable \(u\) the terms after the first two, and their first two derivatives, have modulus at most \(3e^2\): use \(\sum_{k\ge2}(1+k/5)^2e^k<3e^2\). The real part is \(\cos u+e\cos(u+t)+O(3e^2)\), with the indicated absolute error bound. Comparing with \(t=\pi/j\) first gives \(\cos u<-0.9\) at any minimum. Stationarity on the whole circle yields \(|\sin u|\le(1+1/j)e+3e^2\); thus \(u\) is within \(2e\) of an odd multiple of \(\pi\). At such a minimum near \(t=\alpha=(2b+1)\pi/j\), the value is at least \(-1-e\cos\alpha-6e^2\), since the second cosine differs from \(-\cos\alpha\) by at most \(2(1+1/j)e\). At \(t=\pi/j\) the value is at most \(-1-e\cos(\pi/j)+3e^2\). The gap \(\cos(\pi/j)-\cos(3\pi/j)>1/2\) for these four integers, so only the first conjugate pair of neighborhoods is possible. Strict convexity there follows directly from the second derivative bound and \(\cos u<-0.9\) on \(|u-(2b+1)\pi|\le2e\). This yields a unique upper minimum and \(\Re K<0\). The normalized real part of \(H/j\) there is negative by the same first-term estimate.

At \(t=\pi/j\), the normalized imaginary part of \(H/j\) is \(-(1+1/j)e\sin(\pi/j)+O(3e^2)<0\), so the strictly convex minimum is earlier than \(\pi/j\). At the minimum \(\Im H=0\), and the normalized imaginary part of \(B-H/j\) is \(-(e/j)\sin((j+1)t)\) plus an error of size at most \(3e^2/j\), since \(\sum_{k\ge2}k e^k\le3e^2\). Here \(\sin((j+1)t)\le-\sin(\pi/j)+2(1+1/j)e<-0.35\), so \(\Im B>0\).

For \(r\ge64\), \(\left|\sum_{a=0}^{j-1}z^a/a!\right|\le r^7\), and its logarithmic derivatives of order one and two have modulus at most \(49r^7\). On the right semicircle write \(z=x_y+iy\), \(x_y=\sqrt{r^2-y^2}\), and test at \(y=\pi\); \(x_\pi>r-1/8\). The left semicircle has \(\Re B\ge-1-r^7\). For \(y\ge2\pi\) on the right the exponential envelope drops by \(e^{x_\pi}-e^{x_{2\pi}}\ge e^{r-1/8}/(2r)>2r^7\), using \(x_\pi-x_{2\pi}\ge3\pi^2/(2r)\). For \(0\le y\le2\pi\) outside \([3\pi/4,5\pi/4]\), \(\cos y\ge-1/\sqrt2\) gives a strict height gap to the test point as well. The inequalities involving exponentials hold at \(r=64\) and increase in strength thereafter by logarithmic differentiation. On the remaining interval \(0<t<1/8<\pi/j,\ x_y>r-1\), and \(\cos(y+t),\cos(y+2t)<-0.4\). Thus \(\Re H< -r e^{r-1}/3\), \(\Re K<0\) by comparison of \(ze^z,(z+z^2)e^z\) with their polynomial terms. The global upper minimum is unique by strict convexity there. At the minimum \(H\) is real, so \(B=H/z-z^{j-1}/(j-1)!\) gives \(\Im B\ge e^{r-1}\sin t/3-r^7>0\) since \(r\sin t\ge3\pi/4\).

For \(1/16\le r\le64\) the [interval verifier](check_small_threshold_saddles.py) uses the angular enclosure argument of the [threshold-four certificate](../../../snapshot/research/partitions/saddle_certificate.py). On each closed radius interval it finds angle endpoints with \(\Im H\) strictly positive and negative respectively, verifies \(\Re K<0\) throughout the intervening angles and checks that all angles of the upper semicircle outside have strictly larger \(\Re B\) than a test angle inside. Strict convexity gives the unique minimum. A one-sided bisection from each end independently tightens the enclosure of the zero of \(\Im H\), and on that enclosure the verifier proves \(t<\pi/j,\ \Re H<0,\ \Im B>0\). For \(r\le8\) the quantities \(B,H,K,B-H/j\) are divided by the positive \(r^j/j!\) and computed from the Taylor series through excess degree 52. For each real and imaginary remainder the script verifies the rational bound \(2j!(j+53)^2 8^{53}/(j+53)!<10^{-18}\) and that successive ratios in the majorant are below \(1/2\). For \(r\ge8\) it uses \(e^{-r}\) normalization and the exact finite Taylor complements. Both choices give the same signs at 8. Only the proposals for angle brackets use floating point. Proof decisions use Arb, including the comparison with \(1/j\).

Running the verifier on the four thresholds gave respectively 154, 149, 150 and 178 closed radius intervals covering \([1/16,64]\). Each split preserves both endpoints.
