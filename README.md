# MathLab

Research on the zeros of set-partition polynomials and the shifted lonely runner problem by Chengyi Fang, with AI-generated derivations, software, formal proofs and manuscripts.

| Paper | Mathematical question |
|---|---|
| [Zeros of threshold-four set-partition polynomials](papers/partitions/partitions.pdf) | How do all the roots move between rows, including at the two extreme scales? |
| [Compatible replacements](papers/runners/compatible-replacements.pdf) | How many runners can be saved by overlapping local rules at compatible integer scales? |
| [One-runner extension and phase stability](papers/runners/frozen-extension.pdf) | What is the best possible 58th runner when a particular published 57-runner seed is fixed? |
| [A sharp phase neighbourhood for consecutive lonely runners](papers/phases/phases.pdf) | How does loneliness vary with small shifts of the starting positions at prime modulus? |

The [phase proof project](formal-phases) verifies the exact prime-dependent neighbourhood, its sharpness, and the odd-orbit lower bound. The articles include the natural-language proofs. The [downloadable dyadic interval certificate](certificates/partitions) contains all 501,501 roots of the finite partition rows with an independent sign-and-order verifier. The [Lean project](formal) gives the formal statements, pinned dependencies, build instructions and verification scope. Mathematical exchanges, unsuccessful routes and evidence used during discovery are in [the source archive](snapshot). The [independent AI referee reports](editorial/referees) record the inputs that each reviewer actually saw. Each report should be read with the corresponding author response and reproduction evidence.

Use [the partition instructions](papers/partitions/reproduction/README.md), [the runner instructions](papers/runners/README.md) and [the phase verifier](papers/phases/README.md) to reproduce the calculations independently. The exact root computations and the unrestricted optimization over one additional runner answer different mathematical questions from an all-row proof and an unconstrained 58-runner counterexample. The accompanying papers specify the quantifiers.

## Upstream research and contributions

Per Alexandersson's all-threshold project asks for an algebraic invariant that controls the sign at every root. His 24 November 2026 checkpoint already established density one of negative roots at threshold four, the conjugate-saddle analysis on compact radial intervals, and uniqueness of the angular minimum. The MathLab paper treats moving radii, the two edges, the absolute root count and strict interlacing across gaps one through four. The all-row problem remains open. A [finite quantitative check](certificates/partitions/rootwise-margins) converts the published intervals into explicit lower bounds for the project's rootwise sign margin. [RealRooted #1773](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/1773) · [24 November checkpoint](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/1773#issuecomment-7139029318) · [Parent research program](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/601)

For the shifted lonely runner problem, aadrianvv contributed the search and exact verification tools and the configurations for 18–54 runners. tomdif used these tools to extend the range to 57. Cinna Davis reviewed and published the certificates and contributed the Lean formalization. The MathLab extension paper fixes tomdif’s 57-runner witness and optimizes over one additional speed and phase. For consecutive speeds, aadrianvv had already described the complementary-pair obstruction to small phase changes. The MathLab phase paper quantifies this obstruction for primes. [solved-conjectures #110](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/110) · [57-runner certificate](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/660) · [Lean formalization](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/755) · [Earlier phase argument](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/110#issuecomment-7049825074)
