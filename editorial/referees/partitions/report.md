### 1. Pure saddles and the first seam

The angular derivatives of \(\Re B\) are \(-\Im H\) and \(-\Re K\), where \(H=zB'\), \(K=zH'\). Thus negative \(\Re K\) is the *correct* curvature test for a minimum. It is needed on the core, not on the whole circle.

* **Small radii.** In the \(24/\rho^4\) normalization the first neglected terms in the three tail bounds (unweighted, \(k^2\)-weighted, and \(j/4\)-weighted, \(k=4+j\)) are respectively \(\rho^2/30,\ 36\rho^2/30,\ \rho^2/60\). For example, the \(k^2\)-weighted ratio is \(\rho(k+1)/k^2\) and the \(j/4\)-weighted ratio is \(\rho(j+1)/(j(5+j))\). The stated bounds hold through \(1/16\). The comparison outside the two cores works even charging the perturbation twice. In the second core the fifth harmonic has cosine at least \(\cos(13\pi/32)>1/4\), compared with \(-1/\sqrt2\) at the trial point; the possible \(2\rho^2/29\) loss is smaller than that linear gap. In the first core the \(K\) estimate gives strict convexity, and the \(H\) leading term has real part at most \(-4\cos(\pi/8)\), dominating its tail. At the root of \(\Im H\), the first term of normalized \(\Im(B-H/4)\) is \(-(\rho/20)\sin(5u)\ge \rho/80\), larger than \(\rho^2/30\). This establishes all three signs, not just stationarity. Also, normalized \(\Im H(\rho e^{i\pi/4})=-\rho/\sqrt2+O(\rho^2)\) with error bounded by \(2\rho^2\); the core derivative is negative. Thus \(\theta<\pi/4\) strictly.
* **Interval range.** The small-series kernel computes the normalized quantities with coefficients \(24/k!\) times \(1,k,k^2,1-k/4\). The first omitted \(k\) is **35**. A bound for the absolute complex tail, valid for all four, is
  \[
  2\,\frac{24\cdot 35^2\,4^{31}}{35!}<10^{-15},
  \]
  since the majorant ratio is at most \(4\cdot36/35^2\) and decreases. The exterior unit complex factor costs nothing in this bound. Adding the error in **both** components is sufficient. The other kernel is scaled by \(e^{-\rho}\); in particular its `bprime` is \(e^{-\rho}B'\), so that subtracting \(e^{-\rho}z^3/6\) really does give the scaled \(B\).
  
  The core endpoint signs and strict decrease of \(\Im H\) prove unique stationarity there. The outside comparison is with a *trial value*, which is sufficient: the core minimum is at most the trial value. Comparing an entire outside box to an entire trial ball on a radius slab is stronger, not weaker, than comparing at equal radius. The seemingly asymmetric bisections also work. In the first search only the returned **lower** endpoint has to remain a lower bound for every root on the slab; it advances only on a strict positive test. The reverse applies to the returned upper endpoint. One must not claim that both auxiliary search endpoints bracket all the roots at every iteration. The actual enclosure suffices for \(\Re H<0,\ \Im B>0\), and `upper < .25` suffices for lag four. The separate phase brackets are checked inside accepted uniqueness cores. Neither SciPy's selection of its first crossing nor accuracy of its root as a point estimate is a proof assumption on a successful run.
* **Large radii.** At \(y=\pi\), \(x_\pi>\rho-1/5\). On the right with \(y\ge 2\pi\), \(x_\pi-x_{2\pi}\ge3\pi^2/(2\rho)\), so the claimed lower bound \(e^{\rho-1/5}/(2\rho)\) for the loss of exponential envelope is conservative and exceeds \(2M\). Thus both exponential terms in \((z+z^2)e^z\) have strongly negative real parts. They dominate the polynomial in \(K\), and the corresponding estimate for \(H\) gives \(\Re H\le-\rho e^{\rho-1}/3\). Finally \(B=H/z-z^3/6\), evaluated with \(H\) **real**, gives the stated strictly positive lower bound for \(\Im B\). These comparisons work at 32 with room, and the exponential-to-polynomial margins grow. Reflection on all three ranges gives exactly two minima on the full circle.

The branch is smooth because \(\partial_u\Im H=\Re K\ne0\); shared slab endpoints cannot switch global minima in the upper half. Along it,
\[
\rho H_\rho=|K|^2/\Re K<0,\qquad \psi_\tau=-\Im B<0 .
\]
Thus the pure \(t=-n\tau\) coordinate increases with radius. The limits of \(\psi=\Im B/H-\theta\) are \(-\pi/4\) and \(0\). This validates the phase-excursion argument for density one given the certificate.

**Exact middle branch.** It is important that local inversion of \(H\) *alone* does not identify the pure branch. I checked the height argument used before the inversion. For \(N=n-h(p)\), \(P\le p\le r-M\), one has \(N\gtrsim M p^2\). The argument equation for \(y\) has derivative \(1+O(1/p+p^2 e^{-p}+p^2/N)\) and a root near \(\pi\). By \(v=2\pi\) the envelope itself has dropped by \(\gtrsim (1+x)p+\lambda/p=\lambda q\): the cubic in \(A\) loses order \(p\), and the real coordinate loses order \(1/p\). Up to a small fixed angle the envelope continues decreasing. Away from the small-angle region the possible gain from \(A\) is just \(O((1+x)p)\), while the exponential envelope falls by order \(\lambda\). On \(0\le v\le2\pi\) off the primary neighborhood one uses the loss in \(-\cos v\). Taking \(P,M\) large makes these comparisons strict. In the primary neighborhood the angular-arclength second derivative is \(-D/R^2\), with real part \(\le-c\lambda\) and modulus \(O(\lambda)\); the third derivative is \(O(\lambda)\). This proves global pair dominance for the exact circles.

For a genuine first overlap, one can use \(P\le p\le P+1\) and extend the compact pure analysis to a fixed radius greater than all those limiting \(|p+iy|\). Here \(x/n,\lambda/n\) are bounded below by positive constants (depending on \(P\)). On taking subsequences the divided height comparison gives a **global** minimum of \(\Re B\), and the divided saddle equation gives \(H=-1/(x/n)\) in the limit. Global uniqueness and strict radial ordering now identify the certified pure point. Since \(H'=K/z\ne0\), uniformly on this overlap the two points at the *same* \(x/n\) differ by \(O(1/n)\). This argument avoids identifying two possibly unrelated stationary points by their approximate angles.

### 2. Gaussian errors, phases, and degree saturation

All “relative” estimates used in the counting proof should be stated **relative to the positive pair amplitude**, not to the integral. They are absolute errors after normalization, including at zeros.

**Outer cut.** In the small pure radius \(\rho\), curvature at each of the four local height maxima stays nonzero, with bounded normalized derivatives. The height split is \(\gtrsim\rho\) from the fifth harmonic. Integration of the *real* Gaussian bound near the secondary maxima gives \(O(n^{-1/2} e^{n\Re f_* -c n\rho})\), before common factors. The \(n^{-1/2}\) matters: an estimate by length times maximum could fail at the fixed-large cut. No complex stationarity at the secondary maxima is needed. At the primary saddles the full first derivative vanishes, with bounded cubic remainder; rescaling by \(\sqrt n\) gives uniform \(o_n(1)\) normalized error. This works for uniformly bounded, locally equicontinuous normalized insertions. In particular it works for the centered derivative: the exact Euler identity removes the fourth-order term, \(B-H/4=-z^5/480+O(z^6)\), and \(zA'/(4n\tau)=O(\rho^5/n)\). After division by \(\rho^5\) the sine coefficient for the centered \(\tau\)-derivative tends to \(+1/(480\sqrt2)\). Its sign test is for the derivative of the positively rescaled polynomial \(\tau^{-(n+j)/4}L_j[1]\).

The resulting bound is \(o_n(1)+O(e^{-c n\rho})\), **not** \(o_n(1)\) at \(n\rho\) fixed. At the cut in `outer_index.md`, the latter term is \(O(e^{-c'J})\); the constants need not depend on \(J\). The small-radius expansions give \(m\pi+n\psi+\phi=J\pi+o_n(1)\) there. Reciprocation sends the cut to a point with \(X\to(J+s/4)\pi\), strictly between limiting ranks \(J,J+1\) for each residue. Compact convergence and two enclosing circles justify the fixed rank at a *moving* cut. This fixes the offset without approximating the cut integral to vanishing relative error.

In the exact middle range \(\lambda\to\infty\) uniformly: bounded \(p\) forces \(\lambda\to\infty\), and unbounded \(p\) gives \(\lambda\gtrsim Mp\). Even the smaller separation \(\lambda q\ge\max(p,\lambda/p)\ge\sqrt\lambda\) absorbs polynomial losses, including contour length and the global \(O(p)\) sine weight. Locally the lag-\(j\) weight has arclength derivative \(O(j/(R\sin(j\theta)))=O(1/y)\) for these small angles, for fixed \(j\le4\). Gaussian scaling therefore works for the inserted as well as the plain integral.

**Inner cut.** At \(w_*=u+iy\),
\[
S'(w_*)=0,\qquad S''(w_*)=\tfrac32(1-u-iy).
\]
For compact \(y\)-intervals inside \((0,\pi)\), reduction of \(v\) modulo \(2\pi\) and decrease of \(\sin v/v\) give the stated unique pair of height maxima and a uniform Gaussian estimate. At small \(y\), bounding only the original vertical-line Gaussian by its real curvature is not the argument needed. The tilt repairs this. With \(d=V-y\) and \(\delta=1/2\), the real quadratic-and-cubic drop divided by \((3/2)d^2\) is
\[
-\delta y-\gamma d+O(y^2+d^2)
=-(\delta-\gamma)y-\gamma V+O(y^2+d^2),
\qquad \gamma=(3\delta-\delta^3)/6\in(0,\delta).
\]
This is negative with margin \(\gtrsim y+V\) on the stated short ray. On the connector the sign of \(\Re S'\) makes its **original-line** endpoint highest, and the strict inequality \(1-\cos\epsilon<\epsilon^2/2\) gives a fixed gap there in the double-saddle limit. Reflection joins the two deformed half contours at the same point; the change is an analytic deformation in the angular variable, including for the inserted weights.

With \(s'=d\sqrt{ry}\), the near endpoint is \(-\sqrt L\), \(L=ry^3\). The complex Taylor error is \(O(|s'|^3/\sqrt L)\); both the true and quadratic exponential moduli have Gaussian bounds. Thus this polynomial error can be integrated, without assuming the Taylor error is pointwise small on the whole ray. Variation of the sine weight is \(O(|d|/y)=O(|s'|/\sqrt L)\), while that of the \(e^T\) factor costs \(O(|d|)\) locally. The remote \(O(R/y)\) weight is polynomial at the cut. In the \(v\) coordinate the upper tangent for increasing \(V\) is \(1+i\delta\). The square root of \((1+i\delta)^2S''\) in the Gaussian is \((1+i\delta)\sqrt{S''}\), not its negative: its real part is positive, with \(\arg\sqrt{S''}\in(-\pi/4,0)\). The Jacobian cancels the tangent. This verifies the phase \(-\tfrac12\arg S''\) and not that phase plus \(\pi\). The errors are \(O(L^{-1/2})+o_r(1)\), sufficient for \(L=2\pi(H+1/4)\) fixed large.

**Unwrapped seams.** At the first seam \(-xB-n\log z\) is stationary at the pure point, so its *complex value* changes by \(O(n(1/n)^2)\), not \(O(1)\). \(A\) changes by \(O(1/n)\) and \(D/n=-(x/n)K(z_{\rm pure})+O(1/n)\). This proves agreement of the real phases on the stipulated branches.

At the second seam a convenient direct verification uses \(\xi=z-r\). Uniformly on bounded complex neighborhoods, with \(c\) in the fixed seam compact set,
\[
E(r+\xi)-[A(r)-n\log r]=r S_c(\xi)+\xi^2+O(1/r)
\]
also with local derivative control. Use, for example, \(r-M\le p\le r-M+1\) with the middle bounds extended as in the notes. The limiting equation \(S'_c=0\), with \(y\) near \(\pi\), has \(u=y\cot y\), \(c=(3/2)y e^{-u}/\sin y\); positivity of \(c\) selects the side \(y<\pi\). These saddles occupy a compact nondegenerate subarc. Inversion puts \(\xi_{\rm exact}=w_*(c)+O(1/r)\). Consequently the exact upper action has imaginary part \(r\Im S_c(w_*)+\Im w_*^2+O(1/r)\), and \(D/(R^2 r)=S''_c(w_*)+O(1/r)\) (at the saddle \(D=z^2E''\)). This proves agreement as **real numbers** at this seam too. Continuous interpolation with uniformly \(o(1)\) change is legitimate. It is not necessary to interpolate a nearly singular centered weight.

**Counting.** The inner phase tends to \(-H\pi\): the action contributes \(-ry^3/2+o(1)\), and the curvature contributes \(+\pi/4+o(1)\). Also \(c/c_0=1+y^2/2+O(y^4)\), which gives the displayed \(S_H\) in Airy-zero units. The indexed Airy asymptotic strictly brackets \(S_H\) for sufficiently large fixed \(H\). Choose \(J,H\) before the final limit in \(n\). Between the two cuts, consecutive first hits of the interior integer levels, with the cuts appended, give \(m-J-H\) disjoint sign changes. Together with the two edge windows that is \(m\) **distinct** negative roots; positive leading coefficient and degree \(m\) give simplicity and exclude all other roots. For the companion sign, in each such interval take the first hit of \((l+5/6)\pi\) and the last preceding hit of \((l+1/6)\pi\) within the interval. The opposite signs put the unique root in this shorter bracket; there \((-1)^l\sin G\ge1/2\). This also works for the intervals adjoining the displaced endpoint levels. On an overlap the native phase suffices at the root. I find no monotonicity assumption on the composite phase hidden in either step.

### 3. Five-row extension

On compact pure intervals the strict upper angle bound from part 1 gives \(0<j\theta<\pi\) for \(j\le4\). It also suffices as \(\rho\to0\) for \(j\le3\), since \(\theta\to\pi/4\). On the exact and reduced inner contours the angles are small, the local variation bounds above apply to every fixed \(j\le4\), and the upper value of the centered weight is \(-i\). None of this justifies dividing by \(\sin(4\theta)\) uniformly at the far edge.

For that case angular differentiation of \(z^{-n-4}\exp(A-xB)\) gives precisely
\[
(n+4)I[(\rho/z)^4]
=I[(\rho/z)^4 h-x\rho^4 H/z^4].
\]
Writing the right integrand weight divided by \(n+4\) as \(W\), the safe weight is \((W-\Re W(z_*))/\rho\), used at a zero of \(I[1]\). The order-one real constant from \(H/z^4\) cancels on the *whole circle*. The remaining weight and its fixed angular derivatives are \(O(1)\). More precisely,
\[
\frac{\Im W(z_*)}{\rho}
=-\frac{x\rho^4}{n+4}\frac{\sin\theta}{24}
  +O(\rho+1/n)
=-\frac1{4\sqrt2}+O(\rho+1/n).
\]
The \(h\) contribution after normalization is only \(O(1/n)\). Thus the normalized upper value is \(-i b_*\) with \(b_*\) bounded **positively** away from zero on the small-radius part. One may divide once more by \(b_*>0\) to use the same error tolerance as for the other sine tests. Since \(\Re(-i e^{iG})=\sin G\), this gives exactly the desired sign, with \(o_n(1)+O(e^{-c n\rho})\) error, not an uncontrolled difference of two nearly coincident cosine estimates.

For the far fixed ranks I obtain the stated normalization \(c=24/625\) and the limits \(F_s\). The Cauchy circle \(M/m\) gives the geometric majorant with ratio \(625D/M^4\) on \(|u|\le D\). The four \(G_s\) formulas have the correct constants by integration. In the two tangent cases, off both axes the real and imaginary equations would give \(\sin(2x)\sin(2y)=\sinh(2x)\sinh(2y)\), impossible; the poles are not extra roots. On the positive axis the nonzero roots are simple. The four preimages under \(-4z^4\) of a limiting negative zero do **not** give multiplicity four in \(F_s\): the map is locally invertible at each preimage. Rouché counting in the reciprocal disk gives the largest-modulus ranks, not just real zeros detected by sign changes.

For lag four, an unspecified relative \(O(1/m)\) in the original roots would **not** prove the displacement. The expansion in `four_row_window.md` does more. After scaling the auxiliary coefficient variable by \(1/m\), the exponential factor on \(|z|=M\) differs from \(e^{z/5}\) by \(O_M((1+l)/m)\), by the logarithm expansion with bounded exponents. Multiplication by \((m)_l/m^l\) costs \(O(l^2/m)\), and the scaled denominator for degree \(s\) has a nonzero limit and a first-order expansion. For each fixed \(l\) the first-order coefficient has a limit; Cauchy's bound gives the summable majorant \(C(1+l^2)(625D/M^4)^l\) for the **\(m\)-multiplied** difference. The zero extension for \(l>m\) is valid since \(m<l\) there. This proves a common entire first correction for each residue. At a simple reciprocal zero \(u_0\) the correction is \(-G_s(u_0)/(mF'_s(u_0))+o(1/m)\), using \(G_s\) here for the first correction of `four_row_window.md`. Hence the \(1/m\) coefficient in the original magnitude is fixed for \((s,k)\). The ratio at \(m+1\) and \(m\) is \(1+5/m+o(1/m)\). This precision is sufficient; no second-order expansion is required.

The directions work as follows, indexing \(t_k\) from minus infinity.

* With no degree jump, the old outer rank \(k\) comes before the new rank \(k\), which comes before the old \(k+1\), by the strict limiting \(X\)-order.
* With a wrap and different residues, the new \(k\) comes before the old \(k\), which comes before the new \(k+1\); the scale change tends to one. With lag four the first of these inequalities is the displacement just proved, the second follows from the different limiting ranks.
* From the *inner* end the new same rank is closer to zero and the new next-larger magnitude is farther than the old magnitude, regardless of the degree jump. Summing at most four fixed-rank adjacent shifts costs \(O(e^{-r}/r)\), negligible compared with the same-row spacing \( \asymp r^{1/3}e^{-r}\).

One takes a fixed extra rank where a neighbor is used; there is no uniform-in-\(K\) assumption in the edge limits. In the middle \(l=-m+k-1\), so the later-row sign is \((-1)^{m-k+1}\), agreeing with both end counts. There is a later root in every old internal gap **and to the right** of the last old root by positivity at zero. If the later degree is \(m+1\), its sign \((-1)^{m+1}\) at minus infinity also forces a root **to the left** of the first old root. These are exhaustive counts. Thus the equal-degree leftmost root is old, the increased-degree leftmost root is new, and the rightmost root is new in both cases. Lag five cannot have this uniform statement because of degree jumps by two.

### 4. Airy and effective edges
